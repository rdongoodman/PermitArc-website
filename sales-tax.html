Sales tax on PermitArc subscriptions

Listed monthly and annual prices exclude sales tax unless tax is shown at checkout. This page explains when tax applies and how registration by state affects your total.

Not tax advice. Rules vary by state and change over time. Last updated September 2026.

How checkout works: When you subscribe, Stripe Checkout shows your subscription price and, where required, sales tax added on top based on your billing address. You approve the full total before you pay.

Launch phase: PermitArc registers for sales tax state by state as required. Texas is registered first (our business home state). Other states are added when we are legally required to collect there. Until we register in your state, checkout may show no sales tax line even if your state taxes SaaS in general.

Future renewals: If we register in your state after you subscribe, applicable sales tax may be added to future renewals. We update this page when a state moves from “may apply later” to “tax at checkout.”

Common questions

Why isn’t tax on my checkout today?

We only collect sales tax in states where PermitArc is registered with the tax authority. During launch we register gradually. If your state is listed as “may apply on a future renewal,” tax is not collected until we complete registration there.

Will my price go up later?

Your subscription tier price (for example $39/mo Solo) stays as subscribed unless you change plans. Sales tax is separate and may appear on checkout or on a later renewal when we register in your state. Tax is a pass-through to your state — not a PermitArc price increase.

How will I know when tax starts in my state?

We update this page and may email account holders before tax applies to a renewal. At checkout, Stripe always shows the full total before payment.

What about annual billing?

Same rules: listed annual prices exclude tax unless shown at checkout. Tax may apply to the renewal charge when we are registered in your state.

Questions?

Email support@permitarc.com. For official rules, contact your state tax agency or a qualified tax professional.
